ragX: Agentic Retrieval at Scale

Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with one retrieval step works well on small collections, but on millions of documents a question's evidence competes with far more passages for the top slots, and accuracy drops. First, we study why. On nested uniform random samples of Wikipedia (240K→21M passages) that all keep the gold evidence, single-shot retrieval misses the evidence a question does not name 4× faster than the evidence it names; a larger embedder and a deep reranker do not change this, and in an oracle test on two-hop questions a query that names the missing entity removes the drop. Second, we propose ragX, an easy-to-use, open-source and training-free agentic retrieval framework that names missing evidence through two separate routes, a search-and-read agent and a chain of sub-questions, with dense, BM25, reranked and thinking variants. Third, we benchmark it against traditional retrieval (BM25, dense, reranked), agentic methods (ReAct, Self-Ask, IRCoT) and recent systems (DCI-Agent-Lite, Search-R1) with 27 language models on five QA datasets, and ablate what helps an agentic retriever. Finally, on all of Wikipedia ragX leads the strongest single-shot baseline by 8.1 F1, and with a dense index the lead of every iterative method we test grows with the corpus, so small-corpus rankings can reverse. ragX uses 22× fewer prompt tokens than DCI-Agent-Lite, and with BM25 alone it beats every dense single-shot baseline. Masking the names it writes removes most of the gain but only about half of its growth; ReAct given ragX's strategy comes within 1 F1 of it, and Search-R1 leads it at the 7B model size it was trained for.
